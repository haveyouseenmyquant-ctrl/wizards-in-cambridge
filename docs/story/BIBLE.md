# Wizards in Cambridge: Story Bible

This is the canonical story reference for *Wizards in Cambridge*, a browser wizard action RPG set in the real city centre of Cambridge, UK. Anything marked **canon (code)** is already implemented in the game and must not change without a code change to match. Everything else is canon for writing and design, and may be adjusted before it ships.

## Premise and tone

Cambridge keeps time obsessively. Bells sound the quarters from Great St Mary's, college clocks argue with each other across the courts by a few seconds, and water still runs in stone channels along Trumpington Street from a conduit dug in the seventeenth century. Visitors take this as old-fashioned charm. In the game it is the machinery of a promise.

**The premise (canon, code):** the city's bells, clocks and conduits secretly make up a ward called **the Concord**. It was sworn at the first Stourbridge Fair, between the town and the spirits of the fen. The Concord holds back **the Undertide**, which is the drowned, misty fen-time from before the drainage, when the land between Cambridge and the Wash was reed, mere and moving water. The few people who keep the ward working are the **Wardens of the Concord**.

The player is **the Latecomer**, a newcomer who hears a **Thirteenth Chime** after the clocks finish striking. Nobody else hears it. The chime is the Concord calling for help. Someone is silencing the city's hour-bells one by one, and when the last one stops before the Thirteenth Chime is answered, the Undertide returns.

**Tone.** Grounded, wry and autumnal. The game is set in late October, in the weeks when the clocks go back and evening arrives at teatime. The magic is folklore rather than fantasy kitsch: it smells of candle-wax, wet stone, bicycle oil and river weed. Characters are tired, practical people with day jobs who happen to look after something ancient. Jokes are understated, and nobody says "behold". The horror is quiet and old, a mist that comes up from the drains and a light on the water that shouldn't be there.

**Palette.** Each quest runs from dusk into night. The first quests begin in late golden light on Portland stone and Ketton limestone, with cobalt sky and sodium-orange lamps coming on. As the story goes on the evenings get colder and greener: fen-mist, verdigris and the wet black of the Cam. The finale takes place in a drowned blue-grey dark, lit only by lanterns and the player's own spells. Fire stays warm amber, storm is pale silver-violet, stone is ochre and moss, and hex is a sickly marsh green.

**What the game is not.** It isn't a school story. Nobody gets sorted, there are no houses, and there is no secret word for ordinary people. Ordinary Cambridge folk simply don't notice. When they do catch a glimpse, they put it down to tiredness, drink or tourists.

## The hidden Cambridge

**The Concord**

In the game's history, the first Stourbridge Fair was held on the common by the Cam east of town in the early 1200s. Merchants came up the river from King's Lynn, and the fen came with them. The fen spirits, meaning the lantern-lights, the reed-things and the drowned, claimed the land as theirs. The townsfolk made a bargain instead of fighting a war they couldn't win. The town would keep time, measured and rung aloud. Where time is kept, the fen's slow, drowned hours can't rise. In return the town would leave offerings at the fair every September, and would never forget what lay under the fields.

The Concord is the sum of that time-keeping. Every bell that strikes the hour, every clock that is wound, and every channel of running water that is kept clear is a stitch in the ward. Over the centuries the Wardens bound guardians into the most important of these: spirits, constructs and in a few cases willing ghosts, each set to defend its bell.

**The hour-bells**

There are **twelve hour-bells**, one for each hour on the clock face. Only some of them are literal bells:

- **The Market Hill bell.** It is buried under the square, and the town's oldest market rang it. Its guardian is the Bellwarden.
- **The Quarters of Great St Mary's.** These are the tower's peal and the 1793 Cambridge Quarters chime. Their guardian is the Chime-keeper.
- **The Saxon bell of St Bene't's.** It hangs in the oldest tower in the county.
- **The Gatehouse bell of King's Parade.** It is guarded by a heraldic lion.
- **The Mainspring of Trinity Street.** This hour-bell is a clockwork movement rather than a bell.
- **The Round Church sanctus bell.** It sits on Bridge Street.
- **The Conduit bell.** It is a sluice-chime in Hobson's Conduit.
- **The Ferry bell.** It hangs under Magdalene Bridge, where the old hythe stood.
- **The Backs bell.** This one is a drowned bell under the Cam.
- **The Hill bell.** It lies far out on the Gog Magog hills and is guarded by the Grey Knight.
- **Two bells already lost.** They fell silent before the game begins, in towers the Unwinder took quietly over the summer.

When an hour-bell is stopped, its guardian wakes, unbound and confused, and lashes out at the nearest living things. This is why bosses appear in public places. They aren't evil. They are frightened, broken parts of a ward. Defeating one calms it and lets a Warden re-hang its bell.

**The Thirteenth Chime**

The Concord has one more stroke than the clock. The Thirteenth Chime is the oath-stroke from the first fair. It sounds only when the ward is failing, and only someone the Concord chooses can hear it. **Answering** it means ringing it back. The answer can only be rung from the belfry of Great St Mary's, at the Concord's centre, by a Warden who has steadied the bells and carries all four humours of the town. That is the Latecomer's long goal.

**The Wardens of the Concord**

The Wardens are a loose, unglamorous guild. They have no hall, uniform or headmaster. Members include bell-ringers, market stallholders, college porters, clock-menders, a lock-keeper, a retired verger and a man who sells chips on Peas Hill. They meet when they need to, usually in the back of a pub or on the market after the stalls close. They recognise each other by a small brass token shaped like a bell with a wave under it. Membership has thinned over the last century. There are perhaps a dozen active Wardens in the city when the game opens, and most of them are over sixty.

**The Undertide**

The Undertide is the fen as it was before the Dutch engineers drained it in the seventeenth century: endless shallow water, reed beds, peat, mist and lights. It is not just water but *time*: slow, circular, drowned time in which nothing changes and the dead don't leave. When it rises, mist pools in the gutters, the cobbles go soft and black, the reflections in shop windows show reeds, and wisps drift down Sidney Street. At full Undertide, Cambridge becomes an island town again, then a drowned one.

**The four humours of the town**

Wardens draw magic from four **humours of the town (canon, code)**. Each humour maps to one of the four playable classes.

- **Fire: hearth and forge.** Drawn from kitchen ranges, chandlers' candles, smithies, chip fryers and every lamp lit against the dark. **Pyromancers** are warm, stubborn and practical.
- **Storm: weather over the fen.** Drawn from the wind that crosses the flat land from the North Sea, the gusts on the Backs, and the rain that sweeps across Parker's Piece. **Stormcallers** are restless and quick, and listen to the sky.
- **Stone: the city's masonry and its founders.** Drawn from limestone and clunch, college walls, founders' statues and the weight of eight hundred years of building. **Stone Wardens** are patient and protective, and remember things.
- **Hex: fen-lore, curses and lantern-lights.** Drawn from the fen itself, which is the enemy's own medium, turned. **Hex Weavers** are uncanny, clever and a little distrusted, and they deal with the fen on its own terms.

**Places and what they hide**

- **Market Hill (Market Square).** The heart of the old town. The striped awnings hide the iron rings where the first Stourbridge offerings were tied, and the buried Market Hill bell lies under the fountain.
- **The Guildhall.** Its cellars are home to Pip Hobbe and to a dry, candle-lit vault where Wardens store spare bell-ropes.
- **Peas Hill and Petty Cury.** Back routes for Wardens. A chalk mark on a drainpipe in Petty Cury means "mist rising, stay off the river".
- **Rose Crescent.** Its curve was laid out in the game's lore to follow an old watercourse. The Undertide rises here first.
- **Great St Mary's.** The Concord's centre. Its belfry holds the Quarters, and the Thirteenth Chime can only be answered from here.
- **Bene't Street.** St Bene't's Saxon tower stands here, home of Sister Agnes. Underneath the street lies the Ink Hall, the Wardens' hidden archive, entered through a coal-hole by the churchyard wall.
- **King's Parade and Senate House Passage.** The passage is a known "thin place". Its walls whisper at the quarter hours.
- **Trinity Street.** Tamsin Ashdown's clock workshop is here, in a narrow shop-front with three dozen ticking clocks in the window.
- **Sidney Street and Bridge Street.** These lead to the Round Church, whose circular nave is a natural ward-knot.
- **Hobson's Conduit.** Its stone runnels carry the "running water" part of the Concord through the south of the centre.
- **Magdalene Bridge.** The site of the old river crossing and hythe. The Ferry bell hangs beneath the arch.
- **The Backs.** Willows, lawns and the slow Cam behind the college fronts. Mist gathers here every night, and the drowned keep their own hours.
- **Jesus Green and Parker's Piece.** Open ground. Here the sky is big enough for the fen to look in, and the Grey Knight comes when called.

## Villain and stakes

**Corvin Ashdown, the Unwinder (canon, code)**, was a Concord clock-mender, the best of his generation. Twenty years ago, during a winter flood on the Ouse, his wife and young son drowned when their car went off a fen drove into a lode at night. In the inquiry's words, they were "following a light". Corvin knew what kind of light it was. He asked the Wardens to strike back at the fen. They refused, because the Concord is a bargain and not a weapon. He tried anyway, took a ward-clock apart to make a weapon of it, failed, and was expelled.

Grief hollowed him out, and the **Lantern Mother** found the hollow. She promised him that if the Undertide returned, the drowned would walk out of the water. His wife and son would come home, and time would stop on the evening before they died. Corvin doesn't fully believe her. He believes her enough.

He now goes from tower to tower with his old mender's case and a set of keys copied from his niece's workshop. He **unwinds** each hour-bell, stopping its clock, unhooking its clapper or loosening its stay, and he oils every mechanism with a black, fen-smelling oil that numbs the bound guardian before it wakes. That oil is how Pip Hobbe tracks him.

**The Lantern Mother (canon, code)** is the true antagonist. She is the eldest of the fen's lights, the one that the will-o'-wisps and the "lantern men" of East Anglian folklore are only the children of. She was a party to the first Concord and has resented it for eight hundred years. She doesn't hate the town. She simply wants everything to be still, wet and hers.

**Stakes (canon, code):** if every hour-bell falls silent before the Thirteenth Chime is answered, the Undertide returns and Cambridge sinks into the fen-mist for good. Its people won't die so much as slow down and stop, like stopped clocks, and the streets will become a mere of reeds and lights.

## Cast

### The Latecomer (player character)

**Role.** The protagonist. The Latecomer has just arrived in Cambridge for reasons the player never has to explain: a new job, a room above a shop, or a train they nearly missed. They are of the player's chosen class: Pyromancer, Stormcaller, Stone Warden or Hex Weaver. They hear the Thirteenth Chime.
**Look.** Set by the player's class outfit. All four classes share a travel-worn coat and satchel. A Warden's brass bell-and-wave token is added after Quest 1.
**Voice.** Mostly silent. Dialogue choices are short and dry, for example "Is it always like this?", "I'll go." and "Tell me about the oil."
**Where found.** Everywhere. They start at the south-east corner of Market Square at dusk.

### Nell Tapscott

**Role.** Market Square flower-seller, secretly a Warden of the Concord, and the player's first mentor. She has kept the Market Hill bell for thirty years without telling her customers anything.
**Look.** Late fifties. Green waxed jacket, fingerless gloves stained with sap, and secateurs on a lanyard. Grey hair under a knitted hat. Her stall is a riot of chrysanthemums, dahlias and bundled rosemary.
**Voice.** Brisk, warm and unsentimental. She talks to the player the way she'd talk to a new Saturday assistant. "Don't stand there dripping. Hold these." Underneath it she is frightened for the city.
**Where found.** At her stall on the north side of the market.

### Absalom Crane

**Role.** Elderly tower captain of the ringers at Great St Mary's and keeper of the Cambridge Quarters. He is the senior Warden and knew Corvin as a boy.
**Look.** Eighty-one. Tall and stooped, in a tweed jacket worn through at the elbows from bell-ropes. His half-moon glasses hang on a cord, and his hands are huge and calloused.
**Voice.** Slow, precise and full of change-ringing jargon. "We are rung down, Warden. Rung right down." He is fair, and carries guilt about Corvin's expulsion.
**Where found.** On the steps of Great St Mary's, facing the market.

### Pip Hobbe

**Role.** A hob, a fen household spirit, who lives in the Guildhall cellars. He is comic, greedy for candle-ends and fiercely loyal once fed. He is the game's tracker: he can smell the Unwinder's oil.
**Look.** Knee-high. His skin is the colour of peat, his ears are long and tufted like reed-mace, and his eyes shine like a cat's. He wears a waistcoat made from a sugar sack and carries a bag of stolen candle stubs.
**Voice.** Quick, wheedling and full of fen dialect. "That's Unwinder's oil, that is. Stinks of the drove. Got a candle-end for Pip? Little one?" He calls the player "Late'un".
**Where found.** By the Guildhall steps on the market's south side.

### Corvin Ashdown, the Unwinder

**Role.** The villain. A disgraced Concord clock-mender who is stopping the hour-bells to let the Undertide in. The player meets him in person three times: in Quest 5, Quest 8 and the finale.
**Look.** Fifties, gaunt and clean-shaven. He wears a long oilskin coat over a mender's waistcoat with loupe, keys and tiny screwdrivers. His fingertips are stained black with the fen-oil. A pocket-watch, stopped at 9:47, hangs at his chest.
**Voice.** Quiet, courteous and exhausted. He never shouts. "I'm not breaking anything. I'm letting it rest." He speaks of his family in the present tense.
**Where found.** In towers just after a bell stops, at Tamsin's workshop on Trinity Street, on the Backs, and finally in the belfry of Great St Mary's.

### The Lantern Mother

**Role.** The final antagonist: an ancient fen spirit, eldest of the lights, and a party to the first Concord.
**Look.** At a distance, a single steady lantern-glow over water. Close up, a tall figure of mist and reed, with a lantern of greenish light where the face should be. She trails a hem of black water. Her voice sounds as if it comes from under the surface.
**Voice.** Patient, kind-sounding and terrible. She speaks in second person and present tense. "You're tired. Everyone in this town is tired. Lie down in the water. It's warm under the mist."
**Where found.** Heard from Quest 6 onwards and seen in Quest 8. She is fought in the drowned Market Square in the finale.

### Tamsin Ashdown

**Role.** Corvin's niece, an apprentice clockmaker on Trinity Street. She is conflicted. Her uncle taught her everything, and she has started to suspect what he is doing. He copied his keys from her workshop.
**Look.** Mid-twenties. Overalls, a jeweller's loupe pushed up into short dark hair, and a bicycle with a basket full of clock parts.
**Voice.** Fast and defensive, sliding into technical detail when nervous. "It's not a bomb, it's an escapement. Different thing." She is fiercely loyal until she isn't.
**Where found.** At her workshop on Trinity Street. Later she becomes an ally in the Ink Hall.

### Sister Agnes Rook

**Role.** The spirit of a medieval anchoress walled into St Bene't's Saxon tower centuries ago. She is the keeper of the Saxon bell, and she remembers the first fair from the stories her mother told her.
**Look.** A faint, grey-robed woman, seen only through the small arched openings of the tower. Her hands are folded, her face is calm, and her outline flickers like candlelight.
**Voice.** Plain, gentle, old-fashioned English with no "thee" and "thou" parody. "I have kept this bell since before your city had a name for its streets. Sit. Tell me who is ringing badly."
**Where found.** At the base of St Bene't's tower on Bene't Street, from Quest 3 onwards.

### Dr Marisol Penhaligon

**Role.** Keeper of the Ink Hall, a hidden archive under Bene't Street holding the Wardens' records: bell-books, fair-ledgers and the original Concord oath. She is the game's lore source and gives the player sidequests.
**Look.** Forties, Cornish by birth. Cardigan, ink on her cuffs, a head-torch worn without irony, and a pencil behind each ear.
**Voice.** Dry, curious and slightly too pleased by catastrophe as a research topic. "Oh, that's dreadful. Hold still, I want to write down exactly how dreadful."
**Where found.** In the Ink Hall, through the coal-hole by St Bene't's churchyard.

### Wat Gedney

**Role.** A friendly drowned punt-man on the Cam. He went into the river in 1894 and stayed, and he is now the ghost-ferryman of the Backs. He knows every hidden hythe, and he warns the player about the drowned who are not friendly.
**Look.** A lanky Victorian figure in a soaked striped jersey and straw boater. Pondweed hangs at his collar. He stands on a punt that doesn't quite touch the water.
**Voice.** Cheerful, chatty and morbid. "Mind the weir, guv. Took me, it did. Lovely evening for it, mind."
**Where found.** Under Magdalene Bridge and along the Backs, from Quest 7 onwards.

### The Grey Knight of Wandlebury

**Role.** A folklore figure, drawn from Gervase of Tilbury's account of the spectral knight who answers any challenge made by moonlight at the Wandlebury earthwork on the Gog Magog hills. He keeps the Hill bell, the oldest and furthest of the twelve. He tests the player before the finale.
**Look.** A tall rider in grey, unmarked armour on a black horse, with the faint ring of the hill-fort behind him even when he stands on Jesus Green. He has no visible face, only darkness behind the visor.
**Voice.** Few words, formal and archaic in rhythm but not in vocabulary. "You called. I answer. Stand."
**Where found.** On Jesus Green under the full moon in Quest 9, summoned by the old challenge.

### The Shuck

**Role.** An omen and an ambiguous ally: East Anglia's spectral black hound, said in folklore to appear before disaster. Here it is a creature of the Concord rather than the fen, a watchdog that runs the old roads when the ward is failing. It leads the player when they are lost, and it howls before each lost bell.
**Look.** A huge black dog, shaggy and silent-footed, with a single red eye that glows like a coal.
**Voice.** None. It only howls, and its howl is always just out of sight.
**Where found.** Glimpsed at the ends of streets, in Senate House Passage, on Parker's Piece and on Jesus Green.

### Old Mother Gadd

**Role.** A minor recurring Warden. She runs the chip van on Peas Hill and is a Pyromancer of sixty years' standing. She sells healing items ("chips, salt, a word in your ear") and shares gossip.
**Look.** Seventies, in a flowered apron. She keeps a lit cigarette behind her ear that never burns down.
**Voice.** Broad Cambridgeshire. "You look done in, love. Have a chip."
**Where found.** At her van on Peas Hill, open after dusk.

## Main quest outline

The ten main quests move from the centre outwards, ring by ring. Quests 1 and 2 cover Market Square and Great St Mary's. Quests 3 to 5 cover ring 1, the streets around the market. Quests 6 to 9 cover ring 2, the college fronts, the river and the green spaces. Quest 10 brings everything back to the centre. Each quest calms one or more hour-bells. By the finale, eight bells are steady, two were lost before the game began, and the last two are fought over.

### Quest 1: The Thirteenth Chime (Prologue)

**Location.** Market Square (Market Hill), at dusk. **Canon (code), implemented exactly as written.**
**Objectives.**
1. **Reach:** Arrive in the square at dusk and hear a thirteenth stroke after Great St Mary's finishes striking the hour.
2. **Talk:** Talk to Nell Tapscott at her flower stall on the north side of the market.
3. **Reach / Collect:** Go to the market fountain and find a cracked bell-shard humming in the water.
4. **Kill:** Bell-Rope Bat Swarms and a Rooftop Gargoyle, shaken loose by the chime, attack. Drive them off.
5. **Talk:** Take the shard to Pip Hobbe at the Guildhall steps. He smells the Unwinder's oil on it.
6. **Kill (boss):** The Bellwarden of Market Hill wakes in the middle of the market. Defeat it.
7. **Talk:** Return to Nell, who names the player a Warden and points them to Absalom Crane at Great St Mary's.

**Encounters.** Bell-Rope Bat Swarms pouring from the Great St Mary's belfry louvres, and a Rooftop Gargoyle dropping from the parapets above the stalls.
**Boss.** **The Bellwarden of Market Hill**: a bronze bell body on stone-and-iron legs, with clapper-mace arms and a crown of lanterns. Its guardian-spirit wakes from the buried Market Hill bell, numbed by the oil and lashing out. Once defeated, it settles back into the ground and the market stops trembling.
**Reward.** XP, the rare item **Nell's Posy Charm**, and the title **Warden of Market Hill**.
**Hand-off.** Nell gives the player a brass Warden's token. "Absalom will be on the steps. He always is when something's wrong." That leads to Quest 2.

### Quest 2: Rung Down

**Location.** Great St Mary's: the steps, the nave, the ringing chamber and the belfry. The Senate House lawn is visible across King's Parade.
**Objectives.**
1. **Talk:** Find Absalom Crane on the steps of Great St Mary's.
2. **Reach:** Climb the tower stair to the ringing chamber. The Quarters have played only three of their four phrases since sunset.
3. **Kill:** Clear Bell-Rope Bat Swarms in the ringing chamber and Chimney-Soot Sprites in the stair.
4. **Collect:** Find the four missing tune-pins of the Cambridge Quarters chime. Corvin has scattered them across the tower roof among Rooftop Gargoyles.
5. **Kill (boss):** Defeat the Chime-keeper of St Mary's in the belfry.
6. **Talk:** Report to Absalom, who explains the Concord, the twelve hour-bells and the Thirteenth Chime.

**Encounters.** Bell-Rope Bat Swarms, Chimney-Soot Sprites and Rooftop Gargoyles on the leads, with the whole of the market visible below.
**Boss.** **The Chime-keeper of St Mary's**: the guardian of the Quarters, a figure of tangled bell-rope with a sally (the striped woollen grip on a bell-rope) for a heart. It attacks in a rhythm that follows the four phrases of the Quarters. Players who learn the tune can predict its strikes.
**Reward.** XP, the **Ringer's Gloves** (an attack-speed item), and the Quarters ringing in full again.
**Hand-off.** Absalom has noticed that the oldest bell in town, St Bene't's Saxon bell, has been ringing flat. "Go and see the Sister. Take a candle; she likes the light." That leads to Quest 3.

### Quest 3: The Saxon Bell

**Location.** Bene't Street, St Bene't's Church and its Saxon tower, the Ink Hall beneath the street, and the edge of Corpus Christi's Old Court, which is seen from the lane as scenery only.
**Objectives.**
1. **Reach:** Go to St Bene't's churchyard on Bene't Street.
2. **Talk:** Speak to Sister Agnes Rook at the tower base. Pip trades the candle for gossip.
3. **Reach:** Enter the Ink Hall through the coal-hole and meet Dr Marisol Penhaligon.
4. **Kill:** Library Ink Imps have hatched from spilled bell-books and are eating the records. Clear the stacks.
5. **Collect:** Recover three pages of the Saxon bell-book from the imps.
6. **Kill (boss):** Defeat the Blotted Scribe.
7. **Talk:** Return the pages to Marisol and to Sister Agnes.

**Encounters.** Library Ink Imps, Chimney-Soot Sprites in the coal passages, and a Will-o'-the-Cam that has slipped down a drain. Corpus ghost lore runs through the area as ambience: a pale figure at an upper window, and a cold spot in the lane by the old court. It is never explained.
**Boss.** **The Blotted Scribe** (new boss): every crossed-out entry in eight centuries of bell-books, risen as one ink-black figure. It throws curses written backwards and splits into Ink Imps when struck with fire.
**Reward.** XP, the **Anchoress's Taper** (a light source that reveals hidden doors), and access to the Ink Hall as a hub with Marisol's sidequests.
**Hand-off.** The restored pages show that Corvin's keys were cut for the gate locks on King's Parade, and Sister Agnes hears a lion roaring in the stones. That leads to Quest 4.

### Quest 4: The Lion at the Gate

**Location.** King's Parade, Senate House Passage, the Senate House railings, and the King's College gatehouse, which is seen from the street as scenery only.
**Objectives.**
1. **Reach:** Walk King's Parade at nightfall. The Shuck is glimpsed at the far end of Senate House Passage.
2. **Talk:** Speak to Old Mother Gadd at her chip van on Peas Hill about "the big cat on the Parade".
3. **Kill:** Clear Clockwork Porters that Corvin has re-wound to patrol the street and turn people away.
4. **Collect:** Collect three crest-stones knocked from the parapet. Each one is guarded by a Gatehouse Crest Griffin.
5. **Kill (boss):** Defeat the Gatehouse Lion of King's at the gate.
6. **Talk:** Debrief with Nell, who has shut her stall early.

**Encounters.** Clockwork Porters, Gatehouse Crest Griffins, Rooftop Gargoyles, and whispering walls in Senate House Passage that call the player by name.
**Boss.** **The Gatehouse Lion of King's**: a heraldic stone lion grown huge, which steps out of the gate's carved stonework to guard its hour-bell. It charges along the length of King's Parade, and when wounded it roars the Gatehouse bell's note.
**Reward.** XP, the **Crest-Stone Ward** (an armour item), and the Gatehouse bell steadied.
**Hand-off.** The Clockwork Porters were wound with a new key bearing a maker's mark from a Trinity Street workshop, so Pip follows the oil north. That leads to Quest 5.

### Quest 5: The Mainspring

**Location.** Trinity Street and Sidney Street, Tamsin Ashdown's workshop, and the alleys to the Round Church. Great Gate and Gonville and Caius frontages appear as scenery only.
**Objectives.**
1. **Reach:** Follow Pip's nose up Trinity Street.
2. **Talk:** Confront Tamsin Ashdown in her workshop. She is defensive and lies, then breaks down.
3. **Kill:** Clockwork Porters and Mossbound Bicycle-Golems assemble out of the parked bikes on Sidney Street.
4. **Reach:** Reach the Mainspring, a ward-clock hidden behind the workshop's back wall.
5. **Talk:** Meet Corvin for the first time. He has already unwound the Mainspring, and he asks the player to leave.
6. **Kill (boss):** Defeat the Escapement Engine.
7. **Talk:** Tamsin chooses to rewind the Mainspring against her uncle.

**Encounters.** Mossbound Bicycle-Golems, Clockwork Porters and Chimney-Soot Sprites.
**Boss.** **The Escapement Engine** (new boss): the Mainspring's guardian, a brass-and-oak automaton the size of a shop-front. It ticks, and every attack lands on a beat. Stopping it means jamming its escapement wheel at the right moment.
**Reward.** XP, **Tamsin's Loupe** (which reveals weak points), and Tamsin joining as an ally in the Ink Hall.
**Hand-off.** Corvin escapes through the drains. Tamsin warns that his next target is "the water. He always said the conduit was the weak stitch." That leads to Quest 6.

### Quest 6: The Conduit Runs Dry

**Location.** Regent Street, Hobson's Conduit along Trumpington Street, and Parker's Piece, including the lamp at its centre.
**Objectives.**
1. **Talk:** Marisol explains Hobson's Conduit as the "running water" of the Concord.
2. **Reach:** Follow the conduit channels south. They are dry for the first time in four centuries.
3. **Kill:** Clear Will-o'-the-Cam wisps luring pedestrians onto Parker's Piece, and the first Fen Lantern-Hags.
4. **Collect:** Clear three sluice-plates choked with fen-weed.
5. **Kill (boss):** Defeat the Lode-Wyrm.
6. **Reach:** Stand at the lamp in the middle of Parker's Piece and hear the Lantern Mother for the first time.

**Encounters.** Will-o'-the-Cam wisps, Fen Lantern-Hags, Wakened Founder Statues stirring on Regent Street, and the Shuck on the far side of the Piece.
**Boss.** **The Lode-Wyrm** (new boss): a long eel-thing of peat and water that has taken over the conduit's guardian. It burrows under the turf and surfaces in sinkholes.
**Reward.** XP, the **Conduit Flask** (a healing item that refills at running water), and the Conduit bell steadied.
**Hand-off.** The Lantern Mother speaks: "Thank you for the water, little light. I'll come up the river instead." That leads to Quest 7.

### Quest 7: The Ferry Bell

**Location.** Bridge Street, the Round Church, Magdalene Bridge and the old hythe. St John's College gate appears on the way as scenery only.
**Objectives.**
1. **Reach:** Go up Sidney Street to Bridge Street and find the Round Church ringed in mist.
2. **Kill:** Clear Cam Wraiths and Punt-Pole Revenants rising from the river.
3. **Talk:** Steady the Round Church sanctus bell, then meet Wat Gedney under Magdalene Bridge.
4. **Collect:** Collect the four bell-stays of the Ferry bell, which are scattered on the riverbanks.
5. **Kill (boss):** Defeat the Quant-King of Magdalene Bridge.
6. **Talk:** Wat agrees to ferry the player to the Backs.

**Encounters.** Cam Wraiths, Punt-Pole Revenants, Will-o'-the-Cam wisps, and a Mossbound Bicycle-Golem on Bridge Street.
**Boss.** **The Quant-King of Magdalene Bridge** (new boss): a giant drowned ferryman made of every lost punt-pole in the river. ("Quant" is the fen word for a punt-pole.) It sweeps the bridge and the banks.
**Reward.** XP, the **Ferryman's Coin**, which unlocks Wat's river fast-travel, and two bells steadied: the Round Church sanctus bell and the Ferry bell.
**Hand-off.** Wat names the place the drowned go: "The Backs, guv. Under the old bell." That leads to Quest 8.

### Quest 8: The Drowned Bell

**Location.** The Backs, behind Clare, King's and Trinity, with the college fronts and bridges seen from the river as scenery only. The lawns, willows and the Cam are all deep in mist.
**Objectives.**
1. **Reach:** Ride Wat's punt along the Backs at night.
2. **Kill:** Clear the Fen Lantern-Hags gathering on the lawns and Cam Wraiths in the willows.
3. **Talk:** Find Corvin waist-deep in the river, unwinding the Backs bell. The player can choose between Talk and Fight. Both lead on.
4. **Kill (boss):** The Lantern Mother sends the Weir-Widow to protect Corvin. Defeat it.
5. **Talk:** Corvin sees the Lantern Mother's face for the first time and doubts her, but flees.

**Encounters.** Fen Lantern-Hags, Cam Wraiths and Punt-Pole Revenants. The Lantern Mother is visible as a single steady light on the river.
**Boss.** **The Weir-Widow** (new boss): a drowned figure in waterlogged mourning, the Lantern Mother's servant. She drags the player towards the water and calls up wisps.
**Reward.** XP, **Corvin's Stopped Watch** (a key item that holds a memory of 9:47), and the Backs bell lost. The Backs bell is the first one the player fails to save, and this is scripted.
**Hand-off.** Absalom tells the player that only the Hill bell remains far from the city. Its keeper must judge whether the Latecomer is fit to answer the Chime. That leads to Quest 9.

### Quest 9: Knight, Come Forth

**Location.** Jesus Green and Midsummer Common, under a full moon. The Gog Magog hills appear as a faint ring on the south-eastern horizon.
**Objectives.**
1. **Talk:** Marisol finds Gervase of Tilbury's account of the Wandlebury knight in the Ink Hall.
2. **Reach:** Follow the Shuck to the middle of Jesus Green.
3. **Talk:** Speak the old challenge into the moonlight.
4. **Kill (boss):** Duel the Grey Knight of Wandlebury. Win or yield.
5. **Talk:** The Knight surrenders the Hill bell's oath-cord.

**Encounters.** None before the duel. The green is silent apart from the Shuck's howl. Afterwards, Bell-Rope Bat Swarms pour out of the city as the last bells start to stop.
**Boss.** **The Grey Knight of Wandlebury** (new boss): a mounted duel in an arena ringed by spectral earthworks. Following the folklore, the Knight cannot be killed. He yields when beaten, and he also accepts a player who yields with honour, though that brings a smaller reward.
**Reward.** XP, the **Hill Oath-Cord** (the key to the finale), and the **Grey Rider's Mantle** (a rare cloak).
**Hand-off.** As the player returns, every bell in Cambridge stops at once. The Undertide comes up through Market Square. That leads to Quest 10.

### Quest 10: The Thirteenth Hour (Finale)

**Location.** The drowned centre of Cambridge: Market Square under black water, Petty Cury and Rose Crescent turned into reed channels, the Guildhall half-sunk, and finally the belfry of Great St Mary's.
**Objectives.**
1. **Reach:** Wade from Jesus Green back to the market, with Wat's punt on the flooded streets.
2. **Talk:** Rally the Wardens: Nell, Absalom, Pip, Tamsin, Marisol and Mother Gadd, each at their post.
3. **Kill:** Fight through Fen Lantern-Hags, Cam Wraiths and Wakened Founder Statues fighting each other.
4. **Talk / Kill:** Reach the Great St Mary's belfry and face Corvin. With his Stopped Watch, the player can persuade him to stand down. Without it, he must be beaten.
5. **Kill (boss):** Defeat the Lantern Mother in the drowned Market Square.
6. **Reach:** Ring the Thirteenth Chime from the belfry with all four humours.

**Encounters.** Every fen mob in the game.
**Boss.** **The Lantern Mother**, in three phases: the light over the water, the reed-woman, and the drowned square itself. In the final phase the player relights the market's lanterns one by one using the humours.
**Reward.** XP, the title **Warden of the Concord**, and the city restored. The Concord is renewed for another eight centuries. In the epilogue, Corvin rewinds the Backs bell himself under the Wardens' supervision.
**Hand-off.** End of the main story. Post-game, Absalom gives the player a set of "change-ringing" challenges as replayable boss rushes.

## Presentation

**Dialogue boxes.** Dialogue appears in a panel along the bottom of the screen, styled as a sheet of old parchment pinned to slate. Each box holds **at most two lines** of text, about 90 characters per line. Longer speeches split across boxes, and the player advances with Space or a click. Lines are short and spoken, with one idea per box. Example: "Don't stand there dripping. Hold these." / "Right. Now listen for the next stroke."

**Speaker names and portraits.** The speaker's name sits in a small brass plate at the top left of the box. A head-and-shoulders portrait (painted, muted, dusk-lit) appears beside it. Portraits change expression in a small set of moods: neutral, worried, amused and angry. The Lantern Mother's portrait is only a lantern glow.

**Quest tracker.** The top-right tracker shows the current quest title and its next objective in "verb + noun" form, for example "Talk to Nell Tapscott" or "Defeat the Bellwarden of Market Hill". A small bell icon fills in as each hour-bell is steadied. A twelve-segment ring around it shows how many bells still sound, so it doubles as the stakes meter.

**Camera beats and captions.** Story beats use short cutscene-style camera moves:

- **Establishing pans.** On entering a new area the camera eases out, a location caption fades in at the lower left ("Bene't Street — dusk"), and the camera eases back.
- **Boss reveals.** The camera lifts to frame the boss, its name plate slams in at the top of the screen, and then control returns.
- **Bell-stopping stings.** When a distant bell stops, the camera briefly looks towards the tower and shows the caption "The bell at the Round Church has stopped."
- **The Thirteenth Chime.** The screen desaturates for one beat and a single tolling caption appears: "...thirteen."

Cutscenes always last under ten seconds and can always be skipped.

**Tone of lines.** Lines should be wry, specific and local. Characters talk about bus routes, the rain and chip prices as readily as about curses. Keep exposition to Marisol and Absalom. Everyone else talks about what is in front of them. Don't use archaic parody, exclamation marks in villain lines, or the words "chosen one", "prophecy" or "destiny". The Latecomer is chosen by a bell, and that is all.

## Originality checklist

| Element | Checked against | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| The Concord (a ward of bells, clocks and conduits) | Harry Potter, Discworld, Elden Ring | Original. It is built on real Cambridge time-keeping and the historical Stourbridge Fair. |
| Wardens of the Concord (a loose civic guild) | Harry Potter (school, houses, sorting), D&D factions | Original. There is no school, house, sorting or headmaster. Members are tradespeople. |
| No term for non-magic people | Harry Potter | Compliant. Ordinary people are just "townsfolk" or "people". |
| The four humours of the town | D&D elements, Elden Ring, The Witcher signs | Original framing, rooted in the historical idea of the four humours and in local sources. |
| Class names (Pyromancer, Stormcaller, Stone Warden, Hex Weaver) | Dark Souls ("pyromancy"), D&D | Generic descriptive English terms. No borrowed spell lists, items or look. |
| The Undertide | Elden Ring, Dark Souls | Original. Based on the real drainage of the fens. |
| Corvin Ashdown and the Lantern Mother | All franchises | Original characters. The Lantern Mother draws on public-domain fenland "lantern men" folklore. |
| The Grey Knight of Wandlebury | Dark Souls knights, Elden Ring | Public-domain folklore (Gervase of Tilbury, around 1210). The look is generic armour with no franchise styling. |
| The Shuck | Harry Potter ("the Grim") | Based on public-domain East Anglian Black Shuck folklore. It is called "the Shuck", not a "grim", and it is an ally, not a death omen. |
| Pip Hobbe (a hob) | Harry Potter house-elves | Based on English hob folklore. He is free, greedy and paid in candle-ends, not enslaved. He has no elf iconography. |
| Bosses and mobs | All franchises | Original. Each is tied to a real Cambridge place or object. |
| No wands, no broom sport, no spell names borrowed | Harry Potter, Fantastic Beasts | Compliant. Spells are named by humour and effect. |
| Real living people | Real-people rule | Compliant. No living person appears. The Corpus Clock and its creator are not featured. Historical figures (Gervase of Tilbury, Thomas Hobson) are referenced only as long-dead sources and namesakes. |
| Real colleges, churches and shops | Real-institution rule | Scenery only. Colleges are seen as fronts and gates. Guardians are spirits bound into bells, not agents of any institution. No real organisation sponsors, employs or opposes anyone. The Ink Hall and the Wardens are fictional. |
| Corpus ghost legends | Real-institution rule | Ambient folklore only, with no plot role for the college. |
